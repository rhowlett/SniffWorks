## AD2 logic-analyser timing during LB-12

The AD2 sampled DIO0 = SCL and DIO1 = SDA at 20–25 MS/s, triggered on START: 1200 transactions
per pass, two passes.

- SCL high is never below 4.92 µs and SCL low never below 5.12 µs.
- The lows before clocks 8 and 9 and after clock 9 are all 5.12–5.16 µs: **no clock stretch in
  the address byte**.
- SDA setup before clock 9 is ≥ 2.0 µs. SDA never moves while SCL is high.
- No runt pulses below 2 µs.

**Conclusion.** At 100 kHz the bus is clean. H2 (a narrow SCL pulse) is refuted. Each B30 slip
is the C031 sampler missing a full 4.9 µs SCL high: the address-ACK clock (clock 9), so every
data byte arrives shifted left one bit with the ACK as its LSB, e.g. `06 A0` → `0C 41`. That is
a sampler stall or slow iteration (H1). The earlier "slave stretches at the ACK" explanation was
wrong.

## Trace-pin measurement (fw15_trace, SNIFF_TRACE=2)

Jumpers: C031 A0 (PA0, LOOP toggle) → AD2 DIO2; A1 (PA1, BUSY) → AD2 DIO3. Sampled at 25 MS/s.

- **Idle capture loop:** 1.3 µs per iteration (98.5 %). The upkeep iteration every 64th pass
  takes 3.3 µs.
- **During LB-12** (1200 START-triggered windows): every iteration that sees a line change takes
  3.1–3.6 µs (SCL fall or SDA change) or 5.2–5.8 µs (SCL rise), with a tail up to 8.2 µs. BUSY
  (upkeep / frame flush) was low during these gaps.
- **Whole SCL highs with no loop sample:** clocks 4, 7, 9 (×3) and 10 (×2) in one pass. The
  probe flagged 37 FRAMING phases in that capture.
- **Experiment:** reading the timestamp only on edges cut the idle pass to 0.9–1.0 µs, but edge
  passes went to 3.5 / 5.4 µs and FRAMING to 61. Reverted.

**Root cause of B30.** One decode pass (`i2c_dec_sample` + main-loop bookkeeping) costs
~120–220 CPU cycles on the M0+ at 48 MHz, while a 100 kHz SCL high is ~235 cycles. When a decode
pass starts just before a clock rise, the whole high can pass unseen. The polled single loop
cannot have margin at 100 kHz, let alone 400 kHz (B31). Sampling has to be decoupled from
decoding.
